01Economy

Greek public debt and the budget

Greece's public debt fell from a peak of 209.4% of GDP in 2020 to 145.4% in 2025, and the budget swung from a deficit of 9.9% of GDP to a surplus of 2.0% (Eurostat, Oct 2026). Debt in euros barely changed: the ratio fell because nominal GDP grew by about half. The IMF projects 115.3% by 2030.

Greece is growing faster than the euro area

Greece's real GDP grew 2.1% in 2025 against 1.2% for the euro area (Eurostat), the fifth straight year of faster growth. Household spending, investment backed by EU recovery funds, and exports drove it.

Foreign direct investment in Greece

Foreign companies and investors put a net €12.6bn into Greece in 2025, the most in the Bank of Greece's series since 2002 and 68.9% more than in 2024. A third went into electricity. Another €8.3bn arrived in January–September 2026 (provisional).

Long readRead →04Markets

The Athens stock market and developed-market status

The Athens General Index closed at 2,552.70 on 20 Nov 2026, up 479% from its closing low of 440.88 on 11 Feb 2016. It rose 44% in 2025, a fifth positive year in a row, and listed companies were worth €147bn at end-2025. FTSE Russell, S&P Dow Jones Indices and STOXX moved Greece to developed-market status on 21 Sep 2026, and MSCI follows in May 2027.

Long readRead →05Markets

Credit ratings, bond yields and the banks

Greece is rated BBB, investment grade, after more than a decade as junk. Scope and DBRS restored investment grade in 2023, S&P and Fitch followed that year and Moody's in March 2025. Markets moved first: the 10-year spread over Germany averaged 0.78 points in 2025, against 21 points in 2012 (Eurostat), and bad loans fell to 3.4% of bank loans in June 2026, from 46% in 2017.

Long readRead →06Jobs

Jobs, unemployment and wages

Greek unemployment fell from 27.8% in 2013 to 8.9% in 2025, and the monthly rate reached 7.4% in August 2026, the lowest since May 2008 (Eurostat). A record 71.0% of 20–64 year-olds are in work and the minimum wage is €920.

Long readRead →07Talent

Brain drain and brain regain

In 2024, 51,993 Greek citizens settled in Greece and 32,141 left, a net gain of 19,852 and the second positive year in a row, according to ELSTAT estimates published by Eurostat. Departures have more than halved since the 2012 peak of 70,696.

Long readRead →08Tech

Startups and the tech sector

Greek startups raised more than €732m in 2025, about six times the €120m of 2019, according to Found.ation. Microsoft, Google and Data4 are building data centres near Athens, and Greeks now use online public services more than the EU average (61% against 58% in 2025, Eurostat).

Long readRead →09Energy

Greece is almost out of lignite, and now exports electricity

Lignite made 54% of Greek electricity in 2010 and 4.7% in 2025 (Ember). The largest lignite plant, Agios Dimitrios, closed on 15 May 2026, and the last unit is due to stop burning lignite by end-2026. Wind and solar made 40.6% of generation in 2025, against 30.2% in the EU.

Long readRead →10Tourism

Tourism

Foreign visitors spent a record €23.6bn in Greece in 2025, more than double the 2010 figure, and 38m of them came, also a record, according to the Bank of Greece. Receipts rose another 9.7% in January–September 2026.

Long readRead →11Infrastructure

Infrastructure and big projects

Greece opened the full 182 km E65 motorway on 23 Jul 2026 and now runs 18 Thessaloniki Metro stations, a project that cost over €3bn. At Ellinikon, €8bn of investment is planned over about 25 years (EBRD). The EU Recovery Fund, worth €35.95bn to Greece, paid for much of the build-out.

Long readRead →12Health

Health and life expectancy

Greeks now live longer than the average European: life expectancy at birth reached a record 81.9 years in 2024, against 81.5 for the EU (Eurostat). Avoidable deaths are below the EU rate. The health system is being modernised: free cancer and heart screening has reached more than 6 million people, the government says, every adult is enrolled with a personal doctor, and €1.5bn of EU recovery money is going into health.
