Economy

Greek public debt and the budget

Greece's public debt fell from a peak of 209.4% of GDP in 2020 to 145.4% in 2025, and the budget swung from a deficit of 9.9% of GDP to a surplus of 2.0% (Eurostat, Oct 2026). Debt in euros barely changed: the ratio fell because nominal GDP grew by about half. Greece is still the most indebted country in the euro area, and the IMF projects 115.3% by 2030.

Economy

Greece is growing faster than the euro area, but has not yet won back 2008

Greece's real GDP grew 2.1% in 2025 against 1.2% for the euro area (Eurostat), the fifth straight year of faster growth. Household spending, investment backed by EU recovery funds, and exports drove it. The economy is still 13.9% smaller than in 2008, and GDP per head is 69.6% of the EU average. The Commission expects growth to slow to 1.5% by 2027 as recovery money ends.

Investment

Foreign direct investment in Greece

Foreign companies and investors put a net €12.6bn into Greece in 2025, the most in the Bank of Greece's series since 2002 and 68.9% more than in 2024. A third went into electricity. Another €8.3bn arrived in January–September 2026 (provisional). Even so, foreign-owned capital in Greece equals 37% of GDP, well below Portugal (71%) or Spain (59%).

Markets

The Athens stock market and developed-market status

The Athens General Index closed at 2,552.70 on 20 Nov 2026, up 479% from its closing low of 440.88 on 11 Feb 2016. It rose 44% in 2025, a fifth positive year in a row, and listed companies were worth €147bn at end-2025. FTSE Russell, S&P Dow Jones Indices and STOXX moved Greece to developed-market status on 21 Sep 2026, and MSCI follows in May 2027.

Markets

Credit ratings, bond yields and the banks

Greece is rated BBB, investment grade, after more than a decade as junk. Scope and DBRS restored investment grade in 2023, S&P and Fitch followed that year and Moody's in March 2025. Markets moved first: the 10-year spread over Germany averaged 0.78 points in 2025, against 21 points in 2012 (Eurostat), and bad loans fell to 3.4% of bank loans in June 2026, from 46% in 2017.

Jobs

Jobs, unemployment and wages

Greek unemployment fell from 27.8% in 2013 to 8.9% in 2025, and the monthly rate reached 7.4% in August 2026, the lowest since May 2008 (Eurostat). A record 71.0% of 20–64 year-olds are in work and the minimum wage is €920.

Talent

Brain drain and brain regain

In 2024, 51,993 Greek citizens settled in Greece and 32,141 left, a net gain of 19,852 and the second positive year in a row, according to ELSTAT estimates published by Eurostat. Departures have more than halved since the 2012 peak of 70,696. The gain is still small: 2023–24 added about 29,000 Greek citizens, against a net loss of about 328,000 in 2010–2022.

Tech

Startups and the tech sector

Greek startups raised more than €732m in 2025, about six times the €120m of 2019, according to Found.ation. Microsoft, Google and Data4 are building data centres near Athens, and Greeks now use online public services more than the EU average (61% against 58% in 2025, Eurostat). But the ICT sector is still 3.5% of the economy against 5.6% in the EU, and basic digital skills are falling behind.

Energy

Greece is almost out of lignite, and now exports electricity

Lignite made 54% of Greek electricity in 2010 and 4.7% in 2025 (Ember). The largest lignite plant, Agios Dimitrios, closed on 15 May 2026, and the last unit is due to stop burning lignite by end-2026. Wind and solar made 40.6% of generation in 2025, against 30.2% in the EU, but gas rose to 40.3% and the renewables share slipped for a second year.

Tourism

Tourism

Foreign visitors spent a record €23.6bn in Greece in 2025, more than double the 2010 figure, and 38m of them came, also a record, according to the Bank of Greece. Receipts rose another 9.7% in January–September 2026. Most of the growth comes from more visitors rather than bigger spending per trip, which is why the government now talks about value, a longer season and regions beyond the busiest islands.

Infrastructure

Infrastructure and big projects

Greece opened the full 182 km E65 motorway on 23 Jul 2026 and now runs 18 Thessaloniki Metro stations, a project that cost over €3bn. At Ellinikon, €8bn of investment is planned over about 25 years (EBRD). The EU Recovery Fund, worth €35.95bn to Greece, paid for much of the build-out. Other projects lag: Athens Metro Line 4 is now due in 2032, and Eurostat shows construction output still below its 2016 level.

Health

Health and life expectancy

Greeks now live longer than the average European: life expectancy at birth reached a record 81.9 years in 2024, against 81.5 for the EU (Eurostat). Avoidable deaths are below the EU rate. The system behind those numbers is weaker: households pay about 34% of health costs out of pocket, more than double the EU share, and 11.5% of adults reported unmet medical need in 2025, about five times the EU rate.
