Foreign investment in Greece hit a record in 2025, from a low base

Foreign companies and investors put a net €12.6bn into Greece in 2025, the most in the Bank of Greece's series since 2002 and 68.9% more than in 2024. A third went into electricity. Another €8.3bn arrived in January–September 2026 (provisional). Even so, foreign-owned capital in Greece equals 37% of GDP, well below Portugal (71%) or Spain (59%).

Foreign investment reached a record €12.6bn in 2025

Net foreign direct investment into Greece was €12.6bn (€12,554.9m) in 2025, up 68.9% from €7.4bn in 2024 and the highest since the Bank of Greece's series starts in 2002.12

That is about 5% of GDP, using Eurostat's GDP figures for 2025. A decade earlier, in 2015, the year of capital controls and the third bailout, net inflows were €1.1bn.16

The climb was not smooth. Inflows fell in 2020 with the pandemic and again in 2023, and the 2025 jump came in the year Moody's became the last of the major agencies to put Greece back at investment grade.

Most of it was share purchases, not reinvested profits

Takeovers and stake purchases made up 41.0% of the 2025 total, and new equity put into companies another 21.3%. Investment in real estate was 16.4%, profits that foreign-owned firms kept in Greece 15.4%, and loans from parent companies 6.0%.3

What this number doesn't say

A large part of the 2025 jump came from one company changing its legal home rather than from new money. Energy-sector inflows came to €4.2bn, and To Vima, reporting the Bank of Greece data, linked that jump to the restructuring of Metlen and the move of its shares to the London Stock Exchange.7 The total was also revised: the first estimate in February 2026 was about €12.0bn.15

What counts as foreign direct investment?

Foreign direct investment (FDI) is money from abroad that buys a lasting say in a Greek business: by international convention, a stake of at least 10%.3

Buying a few Greek shares on the stock exchange is portfolio investment, not FDI. Building a factory, buying a company or putting capital into a subsidiary is FDI, and so is a buyer from abroad purchasing property in Greece. The Bank of Greece follows the OECD's rulebook, the Benchmark Definition of FDI (fourth edition).

Three ways to measure it

  • Flows: the money that crossed the border in a period. The €12.6bn for 2025 is a flow.
  • Net inflows: new investment minus disinvestment. A foreign owner selling a Greek company to a Greek buyer counts as a minus, which is why some years and some sectors show negative numbers.
  • Stock (or position): the total value of foreign-owned capital in Greece at a point in time, €92.5bn at the end of 2025. It moves with flows and with prices, for example when Greek shares rise.5

The OECD put 2025 inflows at US$12.8bn and UNCTAD's World Investment Report 2026 at US$12.86bn.910 Both use the Bank of Greece data, converted into dollars, so they confirm the euro figure rather than add to it.

€8.3bn already this year

Net foreign direct investment into Greece was €8.3bn in January–September 2026, according to the Bank of Greece's provisional balance-of-payments data published on 20 Nov 2026.4

Two months did much of the work. In January, Italy's UniCredit raised its stake in Alpha Bank from about 20% to about 30%, and inflows that month reached €2.2bn. In May, the Public Power Corporation (PPC) completed a €4.25bn share capital increase, in which CVC Capital Partners said it would put in €1.2bn, and inflows reached €1.7bn.78

  • May 2026PPC's €4.25bn capital increase, with CVC (€1.2bn) and Qatar Investment Authority as an anchor investor; monthly inflows of €1.7bn.727
  • Jan–Jul 2026€7.6bn of net inflows, against €3.9bn in January–July 2025.8
  • Jan–Sep 2026€8.3bn of net inflows (provisional).4

Outside January and May, inflows averaged about €750m a month to July, which is closer to the pace of an ordinary year.8

What this number doesn't say

Monthly figures are provisional and are usually revised when the annual data are finalised. The 2025 total, for example, moved from about €12.0bn to €12.6bn. Comparing nine provisional months of 2026 with the final 2025 total is not like for like, and the Bank of Greece has not yet published a 2026 breakdown by sector or country.

A third of 2025's money went into electricity

Electricity and gas drew €4.2bn of net inflows in 2025, a third of the total, after net outflows in 2023 and 2024.2

  • Real estate: €1.7bn went into real estate companies and €701m into property bought by individuals, together €2.4bn or about a fifth of the total. That was less than the €2.9bn of 2024.2
  • Finance: €1.5bn, after €2.3bn in 2024. Euronext's takeover of the Athens Stock Exchange, which gave it 74.25% in November 2025, falls in this year.23
  • Information and communication: €1.4bn, three times the 2024 figure, almost all of it in software, information services and publishing rather than telecoms.
  • Transport and storage: €1.2bn, including €716m in land transport and €444m in air transport.
  • Manufacturing: only €393m, the smallest since 2020.

Hotels and restaurants drew €230m, a small sum next to the €23.6bn of tourism receipts the country earned in 2025.

In plain terms

Over 2015–2025 as a whole, services took 74% of net FDI, according to Enterprise Greece, the state investment agency. In manufacturing, food and drink was the largest destination by far.33

Where the money comes from depends on how you count

By the country that sent the money, the United Kingdom was the largest source of net inflows in 2025 with €5.4bn, followed by the United States (€1.5bn) and Switzerland (€1.0bn).2

These figures record the "immediate" investor, the company that actually sent the money. That is often a holding company in Cyprus, Luxembourg, the Netherlands or London, owned by someone else. The UK's €5.4bn in 2025 compares with an average of about €200m a year in 2019–2024.

Who ultimately owns foreign capital in Greece

Eurostat also traces the stock of foreign investment back to the "ultimate" owner. On that basis, Switzerland (€10.1bn), the United Kingdom (€9.7bn) and Germany (€8.8bn) lead, and Canada appears with €8.0bn even though almost nothing is routed from Canada directly.5

What this number doesn't say

€4.9bn of the "foreign" stock is ultimately owned from Greece itself. That is Greek money that left and came back through a foreign company, which statisticians call round-tripping. Cyprus and Luxembourg also shrink a lot on the ultimate-owner basis, from €11.2bn to €7.0bn and from €10.8bn to €5.2bn.5

The deals behind the numbers

The largest completed foreign investments of recent years are in ports, energy, banking and digital infrastructure, and the biggest single one is Abu Dhabi's Masdar buying Terna Energy at an enterprise value of €3.2bn.13

Ellinikon: big, but mostly Greek-led

The redevelopment of the former Athens airport at Ellinikon is the country's largest private project, with total investment estimated by the EBRD at €8bn over about 25 years. Its developer, Lamda Development, is a Greek listed company, so most of that money is domestic and spread over decades, and only the foreign part of it (foreign shareholders, partners and buyers) counts as FDI.2122

Why data centres matter

Data4 runs, Microsoft is building and Google has announced cloud capacity in Attica. The sums are modest next to energy deals, but they bring the infrastructure that Greek tech companies and foreign firms with Greek offices rely on. In November 2026 the state's new investment fund took a 23% stake in Data4 Greece, its first digital-infrastructure deal.20

Greece still hosts less foreign capital than its peers

Foreign-owned capital in Greece was worth €92.5bn at the end of 2025, or 37.1% of GDP: nearly triple the 12.6% of 2015, but well below Portugal (70.5%), Spain (58.5%) or Croatia (71.5%).56

For a decade of crisis, foreign companies largely stayed away, so Greece starts from a small base. In 2025 it ranked below most central European and southern peers, and above only Romania, France, Germany and Italy among the countries in the chart below. Large, wealthy economies such as Germany and Italy have low ratios because their own companies own most of their capital.

The flow is now among the EU's highest

Relative to the size of its economy, Greece's 2025 inflow of 5.0% of GDP was the highest in the EU apart from Cyprus, Malta and Ireland, where money passing through holding companies inflates the figures. The EU as a whole recorded 0.9%.16

What outside observers say

  • OECD (May 2026): inflows of US$12.8bn in 2025, up 69%, while Greece's stock of FDI remains "comparatively low" by international standards.9
  • UNCTAD (World Investment Report 2026): a record US$12.86bn in 2025.10
  • EY (Attractiveness Survey Greece 2026, Oct 2026): 47 new foreign investment projects in 2025, up 34%. Of 250 foreign executives surveyed, 55% plan to set up or expand in Greece within a year, the highest share since the survey began in 2019, but 22% said their companies had postponed, cancelled or scaled back plans.11
What this number doesn't say

The stock rose by €21.2bn in 2025, much more than the €12.6bn that flowed in. The difference comes from revaluations, such as higher prices for Greek shares after the Athens market's 44.3% rise in 2025, and other adjustments. The EU average of 77.7% is not a fair benchmark either, because Luxembourg, Cyprus, the Netherlands and Ireland host huge pass-through holdings. And EY's projects are concentrated: To Vima reported that Attica took 79% of new projects.12

Gulf money: one big deal done, much more promised

Of all the Gulf investment announced for Greece since 2022, the only large deal that has closed is Masdar's €3.2bn purchase of Terna Energy; the €15bn UAE pledge and €12.75bn of agreements from October 2026 are intentions, not money received.132425

The Bank of Greece data show the effect of that one deal. Net inflows from the UAE were €2.1bn in 2024, the year Masdar bought its first 70%, against an average of about €60m a year in 2019–2023, and €359m in 2025.2 UAE investors held €2.7bn of the Greek FDI stock at the end of 2025, about 3% of the total.5

What these numbers don't say

A pledge or a memorandum of understanding is a statement of intent. The €15bn pledge came with no list of projects, and the €12.75bn covers agreements across real estate, healthcare, food, renewables and AI infrastructure, most of them non-binding. Neither will appear in the FDI data until money actually moves, and how much does is the thing to track.

What to watch

Whether 2025 was a peak or a new level will depend on index inclusion, life after the EU Recovery Fund and the pace of state asset sales.

  • Q4 2026The end of the Recovery Fund. Greece's allocation from the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility is €36bn; €24.6bn had been paid out by August 2026, and the government aims to absorb the rest by year-end.29 The Bank of Greece expects investment to keep growing, helped by EU structural funds and about €9.8bn of unused Recovery Fund loans available until 2029.30
  • May 2027MSCI's upgrade. MSCI will move Greece to developed-market status at its May 2027 index review, the last of the big index providers to do so.28 That affects share-buying funds (portfolio investment) more than FDI, but it raises the profile of Greek companies with the same boards that approve takeovers.
  • 2026–2027Privatisations and state funds. Growthfund, the state holding company, is still selling assets: its tender for 51% of Lavrio port drew five binding offers in 2025 but has been held up by appeals.3132 The new Hellenic Innovation & Infrastructure Fund co-invests with foreign owners, as with Data4.20
  • OngoingTurning Gulf pledges into deals. Watch for named projects, signed contracts and money in the Bank of Greece's country data, rather than headline totals.
  • Autumn 2027Final 2026 data. The Bank of Greece publishes final annual FDI figures by sector and country in the autumn, as it did for 2025 on 1 Oct 2026.8

Underneath all of this are the slower drivers: a falling public debt ratio, steady growth above the euro-area average and a shrinking pool of bad loans at the banks. Foreign investors watch those as closely as any single deal.
