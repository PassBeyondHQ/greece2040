The Athens General Index closed at 2,552.70 on 20 Nov 2026, up 479% from its closing low of 440.88 on 11 Feb 2016. It rose 44% in 2025, a fifth positive year in a row, and listed companies were worth €147bn at end-2025. Athens shares are up almost six times from their 2016 low

The Athens General Index closed at 2,552.70 on 20 Nov 2026, which is 479% above its closing low of 440.88 on 11 Feb 2016, so shares are worth about 5.8 times what they were at the bottom.12

The General Index, officially the Athex Composite Share Price Index, is the main benchmark of the Athens exchange, which has traded as Euronext Athens since April 2026.30 It is a price index, so it leaves out dividends, and investors who reinvested their dividends did better than the line below shows.3

The index reached 2,738.35 on 30 Sep 2026, its highest close since October 2009. Its level on 20 Nov 2026 was last seen in November 2009.1

Why the market lost 92% between 2007 and 2016

From its October 2007 peak to the February 2016 low, the index fell 91.7%, because the debt crisis wiped out the banks' capital and shareholders were diluted each time the banks were rescued.1

The banks held large amounts of Greek government bonds and lent to an economy in a long, deep recession (see economic growth). When the public debt was restructured in 2012 and loans went bad (see bad loans), their capital disappeared, and recapitalising them meant issuing new shares at very low prices.

  • 31 Oct 2007The index closes at its peak of 5,334.50.1
  • 2013The four big banks are recapitalised. The state's Hellenic Financial Stability Fund (HFSF) puts in about €25bn and private investors about €3bn, according to Reuters.8
  • 11 Jun 2013MSCI announces that Greece will become the first developed market it has ever cut to emerging status, effective in November 2013. After the downgrade, the MSCI Greece standard index held two companies and no banks.1011

In plain terms

A recapitalisation is a bank selling large numbers of new shares to rebuild its capital. When the new shares are sold far below the old price, existing shareholders end up owning a much smaller slice of the bank. Greek banks went through this repeatedly between 2013 and 2015, which is why the banks' share prices, and the index they dominate, fell so far.

2025 brought a 44% gain, the fifth positive year in a row

The index rose 44.3% in 2025 to close the year at 2,120.71, its fifth consecutive annual gain and its best year since 2019, according to Euronext Athens.3

Over 2021–2025 the index rose 162%, and the exchange's banking index rose 342%. The banks led again in 2025, with the banking index up 82.9%, and energy, industrial and infrastructure shares also posted large gains.321 In 2026 the index had gained a further 20.4% by 20 Nov.1

The run from 2021 is unusual for Athens. In 2011–2020 the index fell in five of ten years, and the 2014–2015 and 2018 falls each wiped out the previous gains. The only losing year since 2019 was 2020, when the pandemic hit.

What drove the recovery: bank profits, investment grade and foreign money

The recovery came mainly from the banks returning to profit, Greece regaining an investment-grade credit rating and foreign investors returning, while companies paid out record dividends.

The banks became profitable and private again

Greek banking groups made €4.7bn after tax in 2025, up from €4.2bn in 2024, and their bad-loan ratio fell to 3.3% in December 2025, the lowest since Greece joined the euro, according to the Bank of Greece.20 In 2025 the four large banks paid shareholders for the second year running.3

The state also sold its bank stakes. The HFSF sold 22% of National Bank of Greece for about €1.06bn in November 2023, its whole 27% stake in Piraeus in March 2024, drawing offers of about €11bn, and another 10% of National Bank in October 2024.222324

Investment grade widened the pool of buyers

S&P returned Greece to investment grade on 20 Oct 2023, and Moody's was the last of the big agencies to follow, in March 2025.25 Euronext Athens calls the return to investment grade "a cornerstone" of the 2025 performance, because many large funds may only hold assets from investment-grade countries, and cheaper government borrowing (see bond spreads) lowered the rate used to value Greek companies.3

Foreign investors came back

Foreign investors held a record 68.5% of the market's value at end-2025, worth €82.9bn, and made up 64.1% of trading that year, the highest share since 2014.3

Companies paid out record amounts

Listed companies' cash distributions in 2025 were the highest on record, above the €5.4bn paid in 2007, and Euronext Athens puts the market's distribution yield at 4.4%.3 Several of the largest companies are not banks: PPC, the power company now closing its last lignite plants, is worth about €14bn, and Athens International Airport, which rides on tourism, about €3.9bn.4

Listed companies were worth €147bn at end-2025, the most since June 2008

The total value of companies listed in Athens reached €146.8bn at end-2025, the highest since June 2008, according to Euronext Athens.3

Banks accounted for €49.1bn of that, up 74% in the year, and the other sectors for €97.7bn, up 29.3%. Companies also listed on another exchange, such as Coca-Cola HBC in London, made up €37.1bn, or 25.2% of the total.3 By the end of October 2026 the exchange's monthly bulletin put the value of all listed shares at about €193bn.4

46%

Value of domestic listed companies as a share of GDP in 2025, up from 12% in 2011 (World Bank, from World Federation of Exchanges data).6

What this number doesn't say

The €147bn exchange figure includes companies whose main listing is abroad, so the World Bank's domestic-only figure (US$129bn for 2025) is lower.6

Banks are a third of the market's value and two-thirds of its trading

Banks made up 33.6% of the Athens market's value and 64.0% of its trading at the end of April 2026.5

In the international indices the banks weigh even more, because those indices hold only the largest, most traded shares. National Bank of Greece, Eurobank, Piraeus and Alpha Bank make up about 80% of MSCI's simulated Greece standard index, and financials are about 59% of the broader MSCI Greece index, against 22% of MSCI Europe.13

The rest of the market is more varied than it was a decade ago. PPC, the power company, is now worth about €14bn, and builders of big infrastructure projects such as GEK Terna and Aktor are now worth €4.7bn and €2.7bn.4

Greece is a developed market again at three of the four big index providers

FTSE Russell, S&P Dow Jones Indices and STOXX moved Greece from emerging to developed-market status from 21 Sep 2026, and MSCI decided on 31 Mar 2026 to follow in its May 2027 index review.14161715

Index providers sort countries into developed, emerging and frontier markets using rules on the size of the economy, market size and liquidity, and how easily foreign investors can get money in and out. Greece was developed until the crisis. MSCI downgraded it in 2013, the only time it has ever demoted a developed market, which is why Greece counted as an emerging market in 2015 and 2020.1013

What changes mechanically

  • Passive funds swap holders. Index funds tracking emerging-market benchmarks must sell their Greek shares, and funds tracking developed benchmarks must buy them, on the same day. Trading in Athens reached a record €4.26bn on 18 Sep 2026, the Friday before the change, mostly in the closing auction, above the previous record of about €3.03bn in May 2008.18
  • The weight is small. FTSE projected Greece at 0.072% of its FTSE Developed index and 0.065% of FTSE All-World. In MSCI Emerging Markets, where Greece stays until May 2027, it weighs 0.57%.1413
  • The investor pool gets larger. Far more money tracks or benchmarks against developed indices, and active managers who run European or developed-market funds can now hold Greek shares without stepping outside their mandate.
  • The estimates of flows vary. Protothema cites FTSE-linked passive inflows of about $400m and a JPMorgan estimate of up to $2.8bn across the European index rebalances.19
In plain terms

For Greece, 0.072% of the FTSE Developed index means that for every €1m a fund invests across developed markets, about €720 goes into Greek shares. The status matters less for that sum than for who is allowed to buy at all.

New listings are back, and Euronext now owns the exchange

Companies are listing in Athens again, led by Athens International Airport's €785m IPO in 2024, and the exchange itself is now 74.25%-owned by Euronext, the operator of Paris, Amsterdam and Milan.2629

IPOs

  • Athens International Airport (Feb 2024). The state's privatisation fund sold 30% of the airport at €8.20 a share, the top of the range, raising about €785m. It was the largest Greek IPO since the debt crisis, and the shares began trading on 7 Feb 2024.2627
  • 2025. Four IPOs raised €225m, including Qualco, Alter Ego Media and Fais Group, and total capital raised on the exchange, including corporate bonds, reached €2.5bn.321
  • 2026. Four companies have listed so far: Safe Bulkers, Attica Department Stores, Star Bulk and Capital Maritime Finance. If METKA and Uni Systems complete their listings this year, 2026 will have six, the most since seven in 2005, according to Naftemporiki.28

New listings widen the market beyond the banks, from the airport to shipping, and give the international indices more companies to include. Buying listed shares in this way is portfolio investment, which is counted separately from foreign direct investment, where an investor takes a stake of 10% or more.

Euronext's takeover of the exchange

  • 1 Jul 2025Euronext announces an all-share offer for the Athens exchange group (ATHEX).30
  • 17 Nov 2025The offer closes with 74.25% of ATHEX shares tendered, at one new Euronext share for every 20 ATHEX shares, worth about €310m.2931
  • 24 Nov 2025Settlement. Euronext stays below the 90% needed to squeeze out the remaining shareholders, so the company stays listed in Athens.2914
  • 20 Apr 2026The exchange is rebranded Euronext Athens.30

Euronext targets €12m a year of cost savings by the end of 2028, mainly by moving Greek trading to its Optiq trading platform, and plans a group technology and support centre in Athens.29 For Greek companies the promise is access to Euronext's pool of investors.

A price/earnings ratio (P/E) is the share price divided by a year's profit per share. At 9.1 times, investors are paying less for Greek profits than for most European ones, which is one argument that the rise was earned.

  • The 2007 peak. The index closed at 2,552.70 on 20 Nov 2026, about 52% below its 31 Oct 2007 closing peak of 5,334.50.1
  • The banks' weight. Banks are 33.6% of the market's value and 64.0% of its trading, and the five most traded shares take 65.3% of trading against 32.8% for European peers.5
  • Foreign money. Foreign investors hold 68.5% of the market's value. Net foreign inflows over 2021–2025 were about €1.1bn, and the first four months of 2026 saw a net outflow of €95.9m.35
  • Market depth. Domestic listed companies were worth 46% of GDP in 2025, against 84% in 2007, and Athens is about 42% of GDP against roughly 78% on average for European peers.56
What this page doesn't say

Past returns say nothing about future ones, and this page is not investment advice. A price index that rose 479% from a crisis low can fall sharply, as it did by 92% before.
