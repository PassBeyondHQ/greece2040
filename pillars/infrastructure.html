Greece is finishing big projects again, though not all of them on time

Greece opened the full 182 km E65 motorway on 23 Jul 2026 and now runs 18 Thessaloniki Metro stations, a project that cost over €3bn. At Ellinikon, €8bn of investment is planned over about 25 years (EBRD). The EU Recovery Fund, worth €35.95bn to Greece, paid for much of the build-out. Other projects lag: Athens Metro Line 4 is now due in 2032, and Eurostat shows construction output still below its 2016 level.

A motorway finished: the E65 is open end to end

The E65 Central Greece motorway (182 km) has been fully open since 23 Jul 2026, when its last 46 km section, from Kalambaka to Grevena, opened to traffic.1,2

The E65 runs north from the Athens–Thessaloniki motorway (PATHE) near Lamia, through Thessaly to Trikala and Kalambaka, and joins the Egnatia Odos near Grevena. The full length is 182.1 km, and it puts western Thessaly and western Macedonia on the national motorway grid for the first time. Athens to Grevena now takes about four hours, and the European Commission expected the Lamia–Egnatia drive to fall from about 2 hours 30 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes.1,3

The whole road cost about €1.4bn, a pre-opening estimate, and the final section alone cost €452m.4 It is a concession: the private operator Kentriki Odos, part of GEK Terna, collects the tolls.1,5

Why it took so long

  • 2008The 30-year concession period begins; construction starts in 2009.6
  • 2011Work stops during the debt crisis.6
  • End-2013The concession is amended: the 80 km middle section is built first and both ends are postponed.6
  • 22 Dec 2017Xyniada–Trikala, the middle section, opens.6
  • Apr 2024Most of the road, up to Kalambaka, is open; the last 46 km are funded by the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility.3
  • 23 Jul 2026Kalambaka–Grevena opens and the motorway is complete.1,2

At the opening, the prime minister, Kyriakos Mitsotakis, said: "When we are asked where the Recovery Fund money went... this is where the Recovery Fund money went."2 The E65 is one of several roads finished in 2025–26, alongside the Patras–Pyrgos motorway in the western Peloponnese, which opened in full on 4 Dec 2025.7

What this number doesn't say

The 182 km opened about 18 years after the concession period began. The road is finished, but it is too early for traffic data that would show whether it changes freight routes or regional growth.

Thessaloniki goes underground: 18 stations, decades after work began

Thessaloniki now has 18 metro stations: the base line (9.6 km, 13 stations) opened on 30 Nov 2024 and the Kalamaria extension (4.5 km, 5 stations) on 27 Aug 2026.8,9,10

Greece's second city waited a long time. Work on the main line began in June 2006, and it was slowed by the debt crisis, contractor disputes and major archaeological finds in the city centre: excavation at Venizelou station alone cost more than €130m. The final bill was over €3bn, including about €600m of future interest payments, a figure made public on 20 Nov 2024.11

The city's other big transport job is the Flyover, a 13 km elevated ring road built as a public–private partnership (PPP) for about €478m. It was 62% complete in early September 2026 and the ministry's target is May 2027.13

What this number doesn't say

Ridership is only a first-year figure for the base line. The metro does not yet reach the airport or the western districts: a western extension was announced in May 2024, and the government plans to seek EU regional funds for 2028–2032 to pay for it.11

A new district for Athens on the old airport

Ellinikon, the former Athens airport on the coast south of the city, is being turned into a new district with €8bn of planned investment over about 25 years, according to the EBRD (the developer, Lamda Development, says €8bn+).14

The site covers 6.2 km², with 3.5 km of coastline and a planned 2 km² park, alongside homes, offices, shops and hotels.14 The €8bn is the total that all investors are expected to put in over the project's life, not money already spent. Lamda's own spending on buildings and infrastructure was expected to reach about €1.64bn by the end of 2026.15

Where it stands in November 2026

  • Riviera Tower, 200 m and 50 floors, the tallest building in Greece: completion due in 2027, with the chief executive pointing to May 2027 and first residents from summer 2027.16,17
  • Homes: about 1,000 sold; first handovers due in 2027.16
  • Sales receipts: €1.53bn collected by Feb 2026, about €2.08bn expected by the end of 2026.15
  • Retail: the Riviera Galleria is due to finish in spring 2027, and the larger Ellinikon Mall is under construction.17
  • Public spaces: the sports park opened in July 2026.16
In plain terms

The airport closed in 2001, so the site sat empty for two decades.14 Lamda's own spending to the end of 2026, about €1.64bn, is roughly a fifth of the €8bn, so most of the money is still to come.15

What this number doesn't say

The €8bn is a plan over about 25 years, by many investors. The first homes are not yet occupied, and the project's later phases depend on property demand that nobody can promise.

The Recovery Fund paid for much of the build-out, and its clock runs out in 2026

Greece's plan under the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) is worth €35.95bn, €18.22bn in grants and €17.73bn in loans, and by 23 Nov 2026 the Commission had paid out about €24.6bn of it, or 68.5%.18,19

The RRF is the EU's post-pandemic recovery fund, financed by joint EU borrowing. Each country gets money in instalments once it proves it has hit agreed milestones, such as a finished road section or a passed law. Greece's government says its allocation is the largest of the 27 member states relative to GDP.20 The northern section of the E65 is one of the projects it paid for.3

The 2026 deadline

The rules are strict about time. Milestones had to be met by 31 Aug 2026 and the last payment requests sent by the end of September, and the Facility makes its final payments by the end of 2026.19 Greece filed its last grant and loan requests on 29 Sep 2026.20 On 20 Nov 2026 the Commission gave a positive preliminary assessment to the two outstanding requests, worth €11.32bn, which the government says means the plan is fully implemented; the member states' Economic and Financial Committee has four weeks to give its opinion before the Commission decides, and the finance ministry expects the money by the end of the year.21,22

What this number doesn't say

"Fully implemented" is the government's description, and the last €11.32bn was still unpaid on 23 Nov 2026. To meet the deadline, Greece also moved some projects out of the plan, such as irrigation PPPs and regional civil-protection centres, to other funding sources.23 On loans, about €2bn was reportedly channelled through the Hellenic Development Bank, and To Vima reported that the loan money was used up early through 798 contracts. RRF loans go to private companies' investment projects through banks, not to public works.24

You can track every country's payments on the Commission's Recovery and Resilience Scoreboard.25

Public investment by the Greek state, which includes RRF-funded works, rose from 2.5% of GDP in 2019 to 4.8% in 2025, above the euro-area average of 3.7%.26 All investment, public and private, is still lower than the euro area as a share of the economy: 18.0% of GDP in 2025 against 21.3%.27 The investment pillar covers that gap.

Athens Metro Line 4: the biggest delay

Line 4 is a new line across Athens: its first section runs 12.8 km underground with 15 stations, and the contract signed in June 2021 was worth about €1.2bn.45 In April 2026 the ministry's committee moved the delivery date from 2029 to 3 Sep 2032, the date Elliniko Metro proposed, while the contractors had asked for 2034.29 One tunnel boring machine has been idle since May 2026 and is not expected to restart before early 2027, so the new date is not safe.46

Roads: Egnatia changes hands, Crete starts its motorway

The Egnatia Odos, the 900 km motorway across northern Greece with its vertical links to the borders, was handed to a GEK Terna consortium on 30 Dec 2025, with Egis running operations. The state received €1.275bn upfront, and the concessionaire committed to about €3.8bn of rehabilitation and heavy maintenance over 35 years.28,47 The catch for drivers is tolls: a full-length car trip rose from €24.45 to €30.45 on 1 Jan 2026.48

In Crete, the Northern Road Axis (BOAK) is the island's long-promised motorway. Its 157 km Chania–Heraklion stretch was signed as a 35-year concession worth about €2bn in May 2025, with up to five years for design and building, and GEK Terna, Aktor and Metlen locked their partnership in February 2026. Two eastern sections, Hersonissos–Neapoli and Neapoli–Agios Nikolaos, are being built separately.31,32

Airports: record traffic in Athens, a new airport for Crete

Athens International Airport handled 33,993,746 passengers in 2025, a record and 32.9% more than in 2019.49,50 Growth slowed in 2026, to 4.1% in January–October, partly because of conflict in the Middle East.51,33 The airport has been listed on the Athens stock exchange since February 2024. Its expansion was reworked in September 2026 into a phased plan, with €950m of capacity works by the end of 2030 and the north terminal tender planned for the second half of 2027.33 The tourism pillar covers the visitors behind those numbers.

The new Heraklion airport at Kastelli, built by GEK Terna and India's GMR under a concession, will replace the current Heraklion airport. The transport minister, Christos Dimas, said in October 2026 that it was about 73–74% complete and should be fully operating in November 2028, if construction stays on plan. It is designed for about 10 million passengers a year at first.30,52

Piraeus: record revenue, falling container volumes

The Piraeus Port Authority (PPA), majority owned by China's COSCO, reported record revenue of €250.8m in 2025, up 8.6%, with record cruise traffic, but its net profit slipped 1.5% to €86.2m.37 Container traffic is the weak spot. COSCO's own terminal, Piers II and III, handled 3.97m TEU in 2025, down 6% for the second year running, and the whole port handled 4.64m TEU, down 3.1%, well below 5.65m in 2019.53,38 PPA's nine-month 2026 earnings were lower on weaker container and cruise business.54

Railways after Tempi

On 28 Feb 2023 a passenger train and a freight train collided head-on at Tempi, in Thessaly, and 57 people died. The national air and rail accident investigator (HARSIA) reported in February 2025 that the immediate cause was a wrongly set switch at Larissa, made by a stationmaster working alone, and that the deeper causes were poor training, understaffing and a network without working modern safety systems.55 The criminal trial of 36 defendants opened in Larissa on 23 Mar 2026 and is under way.56

Since then, the state network operator OSE has been merged with two other rail bodies into a new company, Hellenic Railways,57 and in December 2025 the state signed a revised €420m contract with Hellenic Train, owned by Italy's Ferrovie dello Stato. It includes €308m for 23 new electric trains, 12 intercity and 11 suburban, which must be in service by 2027.34

What this section doesn't say

The investigator's report explains what went wrong in the system; it does not assign criminal responsibility, which is for the court in Larissa to decide.

Data centres and cables: the newest layer

Greece's position between Europe, the Middle East and Africa makes it a landing point for power and data cables, and a site for data centres that need both. The real status varies a lot:

  • Operating: Digital Realty's three Athens data centres in Koropi; an Amazon Web Services Local Zone in Athens; and the first 15 MW phase of Data4's campus in Paiania, running since July 2026.58,59,44 Google had no Greek cloud region on its published list in November 2026.60
  • Under construction: Microsoft's Attica cloud region, with Microsoft Greece pointing to early 2028.43,61
  • New state money: on 16 Nov 2026 the Hellenic Innovation and Infrastructure Fund, set up by the Growthfund, took a 23% stake in Data4 Greece, whose majority owner is Brookfield.44,62

On power, the 1,000 MW Crete–Attica link, which cost €1.2bn, began carrying electricity in 2025.39 The Great Sea Interconnector to Cyprus is running late: Turkish naval notices have repeatedly held up its seabed surveys, Meridiam bought a majority stake in August 2026, and the grid operator IPTO now targets the end of 2031.63,40,41,64 The EU also granted €228.9m in November 2026 to connect the Dodecanese islands to the mainland grid.65 The energy pillar covers the grid in depth.

For data, Google's Blue cable (2023) lands at Chania and Meta's 2Africa (2024) at Tympaki in Crete, and the East to Med Corridor to the Gulf is planned for 2027.66 IPTO's telecoms arm announced a fourth fibre route between Crete and Attica, ARTEMIS, in February 2026.67 The tech pillar covers what this means for Greek start-ups.

Construction is back from near zero, but still below 2016

Building permits for new homes rose from a low of 5,500 dwellings in 2015 to 41,700 in 2024, then fell 23.5% to 31,900 in 2025, and construction output is still below its 2016 level.68,69

The scale of the collapse is easy to forget. In 2005 Greece issued permits for 174,400 dwellings, at the height of a pre-crisis building boom, and by 2015 it was 5,500, a fall of 97%. The recovery since then has been steady, and 2024's 41,700 was the highest since 2009.68

The 2025 dip followed a December 2024 ruling by the Council of State, Greece's top administrative court, that the building bonuses of the New Building Regulation (NOK), which allowed taller buildings in return for green or energy-saving features, were unconstitutional.70 Monthly permits were up on a year earlier in January–June 2026, but there is no full-year figure yet.68

Construction output in Greece rose by about half between 2021 and 2025, to an index of 151.7, while the euro area barely moved, to 105.7.69 Civil engineering, which covers roads, rail and pipes and so captures most public works, drove the rise, but it fell 6.4% in 2025.69

What this number doesn't say

The 2025 output index (151.7) is still below 2016 (156.5) and about a quarter of the 2008 peak (593.1). The recovery is real, but from a very low base, and the 2021–2025 rise leaned on EU-funded public works that end in 2026.

Who pays: the Growthfund, concessions and PPPs

Beyond the state budget and EU money, Greek infrastructure is paid for in three ways: concessions, public–private partnerships (PPPs) and the state holding company, the Growthfund.

  • Concession: a private company builds or takes over an asset and recovers its money from users through tolls or fees for a fixed period. The E65, the Egnatia Odos, the BOAK in Crete and the new Kastelli airport all work this way.
  • PPP: a private company builds and maintains an asset, and the state pays it back in instalments over decades. The Thessaloniki Flyover is one.
  • Growthfund: the Hellenic Corporation of Assets and Participations, which owns or holds stakes in ports, utilities and other state assets.

The Growthfund

After absorbing the privatisation agency (HRADF) and the bank rescue fund (HFSF), the Growthfund manages a portfolio of about €12bn across 11 sectors.71 Its own targets, which are forecasts, are €1bn of profit and €0.7bn of dividends to the state over 2025–2027, plus €1bn of projects to tender in 2026.72 In February 2024 HRADF sold its 30% of Athens International Airport on the Athens stock exchange at €8.20 a share, raising about €785m.73

The Growthfund also holds 33% of the Igoumenitsa and Heraklion port authorities and runs the tender for 51% of Lavrio port, which drew five binding offers in June 2025 but had no announced winner by November 2026 after appeals.71,35,36 Its new infrastructure fund (HIIF) started with €300m and made its first deals in November 2026, including the Data4 stake.44

PPPs

In May 2026 the deputy minister responsible, Nikos Papathanasis, said 17 PPP contracts worth more than €3bn were under construction and nine more, worth €900m, were being signed.74 Foreign capital is part of the mix, from COSCO at Piraeus to Meridiam on the Cyprus cable and Brookfield in data centres; the record FDI of 2025 covers the wider picture.

What this number doesn't say

Concessions move the cost from taxpayers to users: Egnatia tolls rose by about a quarter for a full-length car trip on 1 Jan 2026.48 PPP payments are future bills for the state, so a PPP spreads the cost out rather than removing it.

What to watch to 2030

The next five years test whether Greece can keep building after the Recovery Fund ends, and the dates below are the ones to hold it to.

  • Dec 2026The Commission's decision on the final €11.32bn of Recovery Fund money.21
  • Early 2027Whether the idle Line 4 tunnel boring machine restarts.46
  • 2027Riviera Tower at Ellinikon, the Thessaloniki Flyover (due May 2027), the 23 new Hellenic Train trains, and the tender for a new north terminal at Athens airport (second half of 2027).16,13,34,33
  • 2028Microsoft's Attica cloud region (early 2028) and the new Kastelli airport in Crete (November 2028).43,30
  • 2030Athens airport's €950m of capacity works; the BOAK concession allows up to five years for design and construction from 2025.33,31
  • End-2031The Great Sea Interconnector to Cyprus.41
  • Sep 2032Athens Metro Line 4, Section A.29

The wider question is money. Public investment rose to 4.8% of GDP in 2025 with EU help.26 Keeping it there after 2026 will depend on EU cohesion funds, on private concessions and on the room that falling public debt and an investment-grade rating give the state to borrow.
