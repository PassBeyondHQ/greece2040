Greece earns more from tourism every year, mostly because more people come

Foreign visitors spent a record €23.6bn in Greece in 2025, more than double the 2010 figure, and 38m of them came, also a record, according to the Bank of Greece. Receipts rose another 9.7% in January–September 2026. Most of the growth comes from more visitors rather than bigger spending per trip, which is why the government now talks about value, a longer season and regions beyond the busiest islands.

Tourism earns more every year

Foreign visitors spent €23.6bn in Greece in 2025 (€23,627m), a record and 9.4% more than in 2024, according to the Bank of Greece's provisional figures.12

These are travel receipts: what non-residents spend in Greece on hotels, food, transport inside the country, shopping and everything else during their stay. The Bank of Greece measures them for the balance of payments, using its border survey of departing travellers and card and bank data. Air fares paid to airlines are not included, so the figure understates the whole industry.

The trend is long and steady. Receipts were €9.6bn in 2010, in the depths of the debt crisis, and €18.2bn in 2019. The pandemic wiped out three-quarters of that in 2020, when receipts fell to €4.3bn. They recovered to €10.5bn in 2021 and €17.7bn in 2022, and in 2023 they passed the 2019 record for the first time. Each year since has set a new one.

2026 so far: another rise

Receipts reached €21.96bn in January–September 2026, up 9.7% on the same months of 2025 (€20.02bn), and arrivals were up 8.3%.3 That puts 2026 on course for a fourth record year in a row, as Tourism Minister Olga Kefalogianni said in November.14 The months from October to December are the smallest of the year, so a fall large enough to undo the gain is unlikely: INSETE, the research institute of the Greek tourism confederation, calculated that wiping out the January–August gain alone would take a 22.3% drop in receipts from September to December.4

What this number doesn't say

Receipts are in current euros, so part of the rise since 2010 is inflation. They are also gross: some of the money leaves Greece again to pay for imported food, energy and foreign-owned hotel profits. The 2025 figure is provisional and the Bank of Greece revises these numbers. The 2024 first estimate of €21.7bn became about €21.6bn.

A record year for visitors

Greece received 38m visitors from abroad in 2025 (37.98m), 5.6% more than in 2024 and a record, not counting cruise passengers, according to the Bank of Greece.2

That is two and a half times the 15.0m of 2010 and 21% more than the 31.3m of 2019. The count comes from the Bank of Greece's border survey at airports, ports and land crossings. When cruise passengers from the separate cruise survey are added, the total for 2025 was 43.3m.5 On this page we use the series without cruise passengers, because it goes back further and matches the homepage.

Who comes: Germany and the UK lead

Germany sent 5.95m visitors in 2025 and the UK 4.89m, together more than a quarter of the total. Italy, France and the United States follow.2 Visitors from the EU were 59% of arrivals (22.4m, our calculation) but brought 53.7% of receipts, because visitors from further away spend more per trip.5

In 2026 the mix is shifting. In January–August, visitors from Italy rose 22.1% and spent 31.7% more, while France fell on both counts. American arrivals slipped 1.5% but their spending rose 21.0%, so the average American trip brought about €1,380, roughly twice the German average of about €680 (our calculation from the same Bank of Greece release).46

How Greece compares

International arrivals worldwide grew 4% in 2025 to 1.52bn, and 3% in southern Mediterranean Europe, according to UN Tourism.7 Greece grew faster, at 5.6%. In the first half of 2026 world arrivals rose only 0.4%, held back by the Middle East conflict and rising costs, and Europe rose 3%.8

The growth comes from more visitors, not bigger spending per trip

The average visitor spent €546 per trip in 2025, less in money terms than the €640 of 2010, so almost all of the long rise in receipts has come from volume.9

Spending per trip fell through the 2010s, reaching a low of €470 in 2016, while package holidays grew from 4.0m to 10.0m trips a year between 2010 and 2019. It rose in the pandemic years, when far fewer people travelled, and has fallen back since. These figures are before inflation, so in real terms the fall since 2010 is larger.

Trips are also getting shorter. The average stay fell from 6.5 nights in 2023 to 5.6 nights in 2025, while spending per night rose 7.7% to €96.6, so visitors spend more each day but stay fewer days.5

2026: volume again

Spending per trip was almost flat in 2026: down 0.6% in January–June and down 0.3% in January–August, with a fall of 6.2% in June alone and 6.5% in August.64 So the 9–10% rise in receipts came from more arrivals, not bigger spending. INSETE points out that air arrivals rose only 3.4% in January–September while total arrivals rose 10.0% to August, so a large part of the extra volume came by road and sea rather than by air.4

Why this matters

A tourism economy that grows by adding visitors runs into limits: crowded islands, water and housing pressure, and staff shortages. That is the reason for the shift in policy described below. The per-trip figures include cruise passengers, who spend little ashore, so a rise in cruise traffic pulls the average down.

How big is tourism in the Greek economy?

INSETE estimates tourism's direct contribution at €32.4bn, or 13.0% of GDP, in 2025, and its total contribution, including knock-on effects, at 28.7–34.6% of GDP.10

The two numbers measure different things, and people often mix them up:

  • Direct contribution (13.0%): spending by foreign visitors (€23.6bn), air transport (€3.1bn), Greek residents' domestic tourism (€2.5bn), cruise companies (€673m), sea transport (€147m) and the Greek-made share of tourism investment (€2.4bn), set against GDP of €248.4bn. It is a spending total, not value added, so it is larger than a strict national-accounts measure would be.
  • Total contribution (28.7–34.6%, "about 30%"): adds the indirect effect on suppliers (farms, builders, food wholesalers) and the induced effect of tourism wages being spent. INSETE uses multipliers from the research institutes IOBE and KEPE: each €1 of tourism spending adds €1.20–1.65 elsewhere.

Tourism is also an export earner. INSETE says that 85% of tourism revenue comes from foreign visitors and that it covered 70% of Greece's deficit in goods trade in 2025. In the third quarter of 2025, tourism accounted for up to 17.0% of all employment, by INSETE's estimate, which helps explain the summer fall in unemployment.10

On a like-for-like basis, foreign visitors' spending was 9.5% of Greek GDP in 2025. That is close to Portugal (9.4%), well above Spain (6.2%) and below the small island economies of Croatia, Malta and Cyprus.11 Tourism was one of the engines of Greece's economic growth after the pandemic.

What this number doesn't say

Greece does not yet publish a regular official tourism satellite account, the standard national-accounts measure of tourism value added, so the 13.0% and "about 30%" are INSETE estimates, not ELSTAT statistics. A large tourism share is also a concentration risk: a bad season, a pandemic or a regional war hits the whole economy.

The season is longer than in 2010, but the progress has stalled

In 2025, 57.5% of foreign visitors' nights in Greek hotels, rentals and campsites fell outside July and August, up from 54.9% in 2010 but no higher than in 2018–2019.12

The gain since 2010 came from the shoulder months. April, May and October together took 21.8% of foreign nights in 2025, against 18.9% in 2010. June and September stayed at about a third. November to March is still almost empty: those five months took 3.5% of nights in 2025.

The Hellenic Chamber of Hotels' annual survey tells the same story from the supply side. In 2025, 62% of hotels (74% of rooms) opened only for the season, for an average of 5.8 months, the same as in 2024. Occupancy rose 2 points in May to 55% and 1 point in October to 49%, while August slipped from 88% to 86%.13

In plain terms

Foreign visitors spent 28.5m nights in Greek tourist accommodation in August 2025 alone, six times as many as in the five months from November to March combined (4.6m). Lengthening the season is the government's stated priority, with money going into ski resorts, diving parks and marinas.14

What this number doesn't say

Eurostat's nights cover registered accommodation and do not capture all of the short-term rental market. That is one reason the numbers differ from the Bank of Greece's: foreign nights in registered accommodation rose 2.0% in January–August 2026, while border-survey arrivals rose 10.0%.124

Beyond the islands: where the growth is fastest

The South Aegean and Crete still host almost half of all tourist nights, but since 2019 the fastest growth has been on the mainland: East Macedonia and Thrace (+19.9%), the Peloponnese (+18.9%) and Attica (+16.2%).15

Athens: the city break

Athens is a city-break destination in its own right as well as the gateway to the islands. Athens International Airport handled a record 34.0m passengers in 2025, up 6.7%.16 Attica's hotel nights are 16.2% above 2019, and Athens hotels charged an average €195 a night in January–September 2026, up 7.5%, at 77% occupancy, according to GBR Consulting.17 The 2026 picture is mixed: international air arrivals at Athens fell 1.2% in January–September.4

Halkidiki and Thessaloniki

Central Macedonia, which includes the Halkidiki peninsula, takes 12.0% of Greece's tourist nights, more than Attica. Thessaloniki airport handled 7.98m passengers in 2025, up 8.2%, and its international arrivals rose 5.8% in January–September 2026.184

The Peloponnese and Crete

The Peloponnese is home to the Costa Navarino resort complex in Messinia, where Mandarin Oriental opened in 2023. International arrivals at Kalamata airport rose 19.6% in January–September 2026, the fastest of any Greek airport.4 Crete had the most international air arrivals of any region outside Athens, 5.3m in January–September 2026 (+6.2%), and its new Kastelli airport is due to open fully in November 2028, according to the Infrastructure and Transport Minister, Christos Dimas.419 More on the big projects is on our infrastructure page.

Hotels are investing more and international brands are arriving

Greek hotels invested €1.55bn in 2025, up 50% on 2024 and well above the €0.99bn of 2019, according to the Hellenic Chamber of Hotels' annual survey.13

Greece had 10,118 hotels with 450,565 rooms and 902,328 beds in 2025. Five-star hotels are 8.6% of hotels but 24.9% of rooms, because they tend to be large, and 72% of hotels are in the one- to three-star categories. Hotel turnover was €12.5bn, up 8.7%.13

International brands

Only 8% of Greek hotels belong to a chain, but international brands are growing fast. In October 2026 there were 402 internationally branded hotels with 36,834 rooms, up from 352 hotels a year earlier (+14.2%), and a quarter of all five-star hotels carried an international brand, according to GBR Consulting.17 Recent examples, mostly away from the best-known islands:

  • Aug 2023Mandarin Oriental, Costa Navarino, the group's first Greek resort, in Messinia: 99 rooms, including 48 pool villas.20
  • Nov 2023One&Only Aesthesis opens at Glyfada on the Athens Riviera, developed with Grivalia Hospitality, Kerzner's first resort in Greece.21
  • Jun 2024One&Only Kéa Island, a 63-villa resort on Kea, an hour from Athens.22
  • Nov 2026Wyndham Hotels & Resorts signs a franchise for Wyndham Residences Gerakini Halkidiki, 174 furnished villas by the developer Ege Yapı, about an hour from Thessaloniki airport. The value and opening date were not disclosed.23

Hotel money is part of a wider rise in foreign capital: see our page on record foreign direct investment. On the same Athens Riviera coast as One&Only Aesthesis, the Ellinikon redevelopment is under way.

What this number doesn't say

The hotel investment figure comes from a weighted survey of hoteliers, not from official accounts, and it includes renovation as well as new building. Brand deals like Wyndham's are franchises: the brand lends its name and booking system, and the developer carries the risk.

From more visitors to more value: what the state is doing

Since 2024 the government has added fees on cruise passengers and overnight stays, capped cruise visitors on Santorini, frozen new short-term rentals in central Athens and set rules on where new hotels may be built.

The aim, in the words of Tourism Minister Olga Kefalogianni, is to meet new challenges "with measure, balance, and sustainability".24 INSETE puts it more bluntly: the focus should be on "improving revenue per visitor and not the number of arrivals".25 In November 2025 Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis announced a five-year national tourism plan for 2030–2035.24

  • 1 Jan 2024A "climate crisis resilience fee" replaces the old overnight stay tax, charged per room per night. It raised €368.9m in 2024, according to tax authority data cited in Parliament.2627
  • 19 Sep 2024Santorini's port authority caps cruise visitors at 8,000 a day for 2025 and 2026.28
  • 5 Dec 2024Law 5162/2024 creates a cruise passenger fee and raises the resilience fee from 2025.29
  • 1 Jan 2025No new short-term rental registrations in Athens' first, second and third districts, later extended to 31 Dec 2026.30
  • 21 Jul 2025The cruise fee takes effect: €20 per passenger in June–September at Santorini and Mykonos, €5 at other ports.31
  • 1 Jul 2026The rental freeze extends to Thessaloniki's first district.32
  • Aug 2026A special spatial framework for tourism bans new building within 25 metres of the shore and sets minimum plot sizes for new hotels outside towns, as reported by travel.gr.33

The resilience fee ranges from €2 a night in April–October for a one- or two-star hotel room to €15 for a five-star room or a villa, with lower winter rates.26 Cruise fee revenue is split three ways, between the port municipalities, the Shipping Ministry and the Tourism Ministry.29 The new fees add to the state revenue behind Greece's budget surplus.

What isn't clear yet

The resilience fee was presented as money for disaster prevention and recovery, but the opposition party PASOK complained in Parliament in April 2026 that there is no transparent account of which projects it has funded.27 The fees are also too new to show whether they have changed where and when people travel.

What to watch

Whether 2026 closes as a fourth record year, whether spending per trip starts to rise, and whether the new rules spread visitors in time and space.

  • The full-year 2026 receipts. The Bank of Greece published the December 2025 figures in late February 2026, so the 2026 total should follow on a similar timetable.2
  • Spending per trip and length of stay. The policy goal is value, so the test is whether these rise, not arrivals.
  • The shoulder months. The share of nights outside July and August has not moved since 2018.
  • Santorini and Mykonos, the first to feel the higher cruise fee and, on Santorini, the cap. They were also the only two of Fraport Greece's 14 regional airports with fewer passengers in 2025.18
  • Housing and staff. Short-term rental limits in Athens and Thessaloniki, and hotels' ability to hire. Tourism jobs are part of our employment story.
  • The 2030–2035 national tourism plan and the targets it sets.
  • New capacity: Kastelli airport in Crete (due November 2028) and the hotel pipeline in Athens, Halkidiki and the Peloponnese. Our 2026 investment page tracks the money.
  • Shocks. UN Tourism cut its 2026 world forecast to 1–2% growth because of the Middle East conflict and costs.8
