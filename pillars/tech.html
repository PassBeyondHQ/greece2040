Greek startups raise six times more than in 2019

Greek startups raised more than €732m in 2025, about six times the €120m of 2019, according to Found.ation. Microsoft, Google and Data4 are building data centres near Athens, and Greeks now use online public services more than the EU average (61% against 58% in 2025, Eurostat).

Startup funding is six times its 2019 level

Greek startups raised more than €732m in 2025, against more than €120m in 2019, according to the annual counts of Found.ation, an Athens accelerator that publishes the annual Startups in Greece report.1,3

The money came in 95 rounds to more than 90 companies, from at least 143 investors. A third of those investors were based in the United States, the largest single group, ahead of Greece (27%) and the rest of the EU (26%).1,2

How the number moved

  • 2019–2020: €120m and €150m, equity rounds only.
  • 2021: a jump to €670m, the first year to include debt, helped by a handful of very large rounds.3
  • 2022–2023: €632m, then €485m as global venture money tightened. Found.ation notes the 2023 total was up 4.2% if debt financing is left out.3
  • 2024–2025: €555m, then €732m, the highest on Found.ation's count.4,1

Most deals are small, and most money goes to a few large ones. In 2025 pre-seed and seed rounds made up about three-quarters of the deals but only €117.5m, while Series A and later rounds took €614.1m.1

What this number doesn't say

The series is not one consistent measure. Up to 2020 it counts equity only, and from 2021 it includes debt, so Found.ation's own report gives two figures for 2022: €632m with debt and more than €432m without. Every figure is a minimum, because many funds don't publish their deal sizes.3

The exits that showed it could be done

A run of sales between 2020 and 2024 showed international buyers will pay for Greek-founded companies.

An exit is the sale or listing that lets founders and investors cash out. Exits matter more than funding rounds, because the money they return is what pays for the next generation of funds and founders.

Instashop tops Found.ation's ranking of startup exits: Delivery Hero valued it at $360m in August 2020, which Found.ation records as €307m. The largest Greek-linked deal of any kind on Found.ation's count is Cadence's $1.24bn purchase of BETA CAE Systems in 2024, a mature, Switzerland-registered company rather than a young startup.6,5,9

J.P. Morgan's purchase of 48.5% of Viva Wallet, closed on 20 Dec 2022, made Viva Greece's first unicorn (a private company valued at $1bn or more), according to Found.ation. Neither side published the price.7,3

The big names that haven't sold

  • Blueground, the furnished-rentals company founded in 2013 and now based in New York, raised a $140m Series C in 2021, reportedly at a $750m valuation, and a $45m Series D in March 2024. It manages 15,000 apartments in 17 countries.10
  • Workable, the recruitment-software company, is still private. Found.ation lists it among the ten most-funded Greek startups, with about €74m raised.3
  • Skroutz, the price-comparison and e-commerce site, is also on that top-ten list.3

Public money built the venture market

Greece's venture-capital industry was largely started with EU and state money, through the EquiFund programme run with the European Investment Fund (EIF).

EquiFund began with €200m of EU and national funds plus €60m from the EIF, and was meant to reach about €500m once private investors joined. It backed nine Greek venture funds, including Marathon VC, VentureFriends, Uni.Fund and Velocity.Partners.3,11

137

companies backed by EquiFund, with more than €350m allocated. Ten sales from its portfolio returned €30.9m to the state.3

Its successor programmes run through the Hellenic Development Bank of Investments (HDBI), which backed most of the record 18 venture funds active in 2025.12 The next step is later-stage money. On 9 Nov 2026 Greece committed €120m to the EIB Group's European Tech Champions Initiative, a fund-of-funds whose first close reached about €2bn with five other countries.13

How many startups are there?

The official registry, Elevate Greece, was set up under Law 4712/2020 and gives registered companies tax breaks: angel investors can deduct 50% of what they put in, up to €100,000 per company. Counts of registered firms differ by source, so treat any single number with care.15

  • The registry's own homepage says "over 500".14
  • The Ministry of Development told Parliament on 2 Jun 2026 that 854 companies were registered.15
  • Greek City Times reported on 20 Nov 2026 that the registry had passed 1,000.16

Big tech is building in Greece

Microsoft, Google, Data4 and Amazon have all committed to cloud infrastructure in Attica.

A cloud region is a cluster of data centres that lets companies and the state keep and process their data inside Greece. That matters for banks, hospitals and public agencies bound by EU data rules, and it is part of the wider wave of foreign direct investment into Greece.

Microsoft announced its region on 5 Oct 2020, calling it its largest investment in 28 years in Greece, with a plan to train 100,000 people in digital skills by 2025.17 The plan it filed with the Development Ministry budgets €976m for the three data centres. Construction is under way, and Microsoft's stated goal is to deliver the region in early 2028.18,19

When Google announced its region in September 2022, Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said it could add €2.2bn to the economy and 20,000 jobs by 2030. That is a government projection, and the region has not opened.20

Microsoft expects more than 340 construction jobs at peak and about 50 staff per building once running.19 The larger effect is on what Greek firms can build on top, and on whether more companies follow Pfizer, Cisco and others in placing engineering teams in Greece, which feeds the return of Greeks from abroad.

Greeks now use online public services more than the EU average

In 2025, 61% of Greeks aged 16–74 dealt with a public authority online, against 58% across the EU, according to Eurostat's household survey.29

The driver is gov.gr, the single state portal launched in March 2020 with 501 services. By the end of 2024 it hosted 2,025 services from 22 ministries and 99 agencies, according to Digital Governance Minister Dimitris Papastergiou.28

Greece was above the EU average in 2022, 2023 and 2025. The 2024 dip to 52.2% followed by a rebound to 61.1% looks more like survey noise than a real change in behaviour.29

What this number doesn't say

Eurostat changed this indicator in 2022, so the line can't be joined to the older series (55% in 2021 on the old basis).

The tech sector has grown since 2019

Information and communication made up 3.5% of Greece's value added in 2025, up from 3.2% in 2019.30

The ICT sector here is Eurostat's "information and communication" industry: telecoms, software and IT services, publishing and broadcasting. Value added is what an industry contributes to economic output after its costs, so this is a measure of size.

Greece's share rose from 3.2% in 2019 to 3.9% in 2020, partly because the rest of the economy shrank during the pandemic, then slipped back to 3.5%.30

Jobs

The ICT sector employed 2.5% of Greek workers in 2025, up from 2.2% in 2019.32

AI and data centres: Greeks use generative AI more than the EU average

In 2025, 44.1% of Greeks used generative AI tools, well above the EU's 32.7%.34,35

Company use of AI rose from 2.6% in 2021 to 9.8% in 2024, then dipped to 8.9% in 2025.35,36

The public infrastructure

  • Daedalus, Greece's most powerful supercomputer (85.69 petaflops, Nvidia Grace Hopper chips), was inaugurated at Lavrio on 19 Oct 2026. It cost €58.9m, paid by the EU recovery fund and the EuroHPC joint undertaking.37
  • Pharos, the national AI factory built on Daedalus, is one of the EU's first seven. Business services are due in March 2027, with free computing time for startups and small firms within EU state-aid limits.37
  • OpenAI for Greece, a memorandum signed on 5 Sep 2025, brings ChatGPT Edu to secondary schools and an accelerator for Greek AI startups run with Endeavor Greece.38
  • Rules: on 22 Nov 2026 the Labour Ministry tabled Greece's first law on AI and algorithmic management at work, requiring human oversight of major decisions about workers.39

Data centres: from a few dozen megawatts to a gigawatt?

Projects in the pipeline would take Greece's data-centre capacity from a few dozen megawatts past 1 gigawatt before 2030 if they all go ahead, according to a To Vima report in January 2026.19 Besides Microsoft and Data4, these include a €150m first phase by Data in Scale (DAMAC with the Public Power Corporation) and a planned PPC campus in Western Macedonia of 300 MW.19

The state is now co-investing: on 16 Nov 2026 the Hellenic Innovation & Infrastructure Fund took a 23% stake in Data4 Greece, its first digital-infrastructure deal.24

What to watch

The next two years will show whether the funding and the data centres turn into a bigger tech sector.

  • Exits: Found.ation counted eight exits in 2025, all small, and calls large exits the ecosystem's main challenge; a large sale or listing would recycle capital into the next funds, and the Athens exchange's move toward developed-market status could make a local listing more realistic.2
  • The 2026 funding total, which Found.ation usually publishes in December: watch the total without the single largest round, and the share of debt.
  • Data centres switching on: Data4's first Paiania building (targeted for end-2026 to early 2027) and Microsoft's region (early 2028, more than seven years after its announcement); Google's region has no launch date.171819
  • Power and water: data centres need both, and the price and supply of clean power will decide how many of the pipeline projects get built.
  • Sector size: information and communication was 3.5% of value added in 2025 against 5.6% in the EU, a gap that grew from 1.8 to 2.1 points after 2019; a sustained move above 4% would be the first sign it is closing.30
  • Tech jobs: the ICT sector employed 2.5% of Greek workers in 2025 against 3.5% in the EU, and ICT specialists were 2.5% of employment against 5.0%.3132
  • Skills: 51.0% of Greeks aged 16–74 had at least basic digital skills in 2025, down from 52.5% in 2021, against an EU average of 60.4%; Eurostat's next survey, for 2027, will show the direction.33
  • Company AI: 8.9% of Greek firms used any AI in 2025 against 20.0% in the EU; Pharos business services, due in March 2027, offer free computing time to startups and small firms.343537
  • Talent: whether more Greek engineers come home, tracked in talent pillar.
